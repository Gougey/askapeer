Six things I need from you

Roughly in order of how much damage getting them wrong would do.

1

Is generic de-identification enough for sports medicine?

The checklist bans names, places, dates and club names. It says nothing about the combination — and in sport the clinical facts alone can name someone:

23-year-old male · Championship-level footballer · ACL rupture · 3 weeks ago

Not one banned item, and possibly one identifiable person, findable in the press. The higher the level, the smaller the population.

Does the checklist need a sport-specific item — no combination of sport, level and timing that could identify someone? And should elite sport be handled differently from recreational?

2

What are the right age bands?

We have committed to a fixed selector with no free date field. The bands shown are my guess. Decades are the norm in general medicine, but an adolescent case turns on 14 versus 17 — skeletal maturity, growth plates — so decade bands may destroy the clinical value in exactly the cases where it matters most.

Narrow bands for under-18s and wider above, or something else entirely?

3

Are these the right nine fields?

They come from the PRD, not from clinical practice. Two things I would expect a sports clinician to want are missing: mechanism of injury and imaging or investigations.

What would you add, cut, or rename? This is the cheapest question to answer now and the most expensive to change once it is built.

4

What does the timeline count from?

The spec says “X weeks post-injury”. Overuse and chronic presentations have no injury event to count from.

What should the options be — post-injury, post-surgery, post-onset, post-presentation?

5

Consent, and who gets to read these

Does a practitioner need the patient’s consent to discuss a properly de-identified case, and should that be a seventh checklist item? My understanding is that UK guidance generally permits it without explicit consent — I would not build on that without you confirming the HCPC expectation and what practitioners actually do.

Also: every verified member can read these. Comfortable with that?

6

Would we really make the referral?

The attestation commits us to referring a breach to the member’s professional regulatory body. That is a strong promise, and it is the sentence doing most of the work — it is why people will read the checklist rather than tick through it.

Is that the right threshold, and would we actually do it? An attestation nobody would act on is worse than a softer one we would.

Already decided — no need to reopen

  • Text only at launch, so no photographs and no EXIF problem.
  • Six checklist items rather than eight, for that reason.
  • The attestation wording itself, from the privacy policy.
  • A moderator can send a case back for correction without losing its replies or kudos.
  • Drafts are private — moderators cannot see a case before it is published.
