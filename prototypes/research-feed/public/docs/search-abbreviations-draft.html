Search abbreviations — what still needs your decision

For: Andrew to review · Drafted: 21 October 2026 · Revised: 29 October 2026 · Status: the agreed pairs are built; the rows below are waiting for you

You were sent 324 short form ↔︎ full term pairs. Of the 291 we proposed to include, 285 are now built into the app (the other six are in section 1): typing rehab finds papers that say rehabilitation, ACL finds anterior cruciate ligament, and so on, in Discussions, Papers and My Research. This page now shows only the 36 rows that need a decision from you.

One change from the draft. The draft said searching either form would find papers using either. That is still true for alternative names (Patrick’s test ↔︎ FABER test), but an abbreviation now works one way only: typing ACL also finds “anterior cruciate ligament”, while typing anterior cruciate ligament is unchanged. The other direction added almost nothing, because papers spell an abbreviation out beside it, and it was where the wrong matches came from: search ignores capitals, so linking TENS back made transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation also find every paper that says “tens of thousands”. So the question for each row below is just: should typing the short form also find papers on the full term?

How to reply: for each row, include or leave out. Anything you don’t mention stays out. And please add any shorthand you and your colleagues type that is missing; adding a pair later is a small change, not a rebuild.

The numbers are from the papers on live on 21 October. “3,171 → 13,949” means typing the short form finds 3,171 papers today and would find 13,949 with the link.
