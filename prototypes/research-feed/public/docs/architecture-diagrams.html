Three views of what we have built

Askapeer is a verified-only, pseudonymous professional network for sports medicine practitioners. Every member is a registered clinician; no one can see whether the answer came from a consultant of thirty years or a first-year graduate. These three diagrams show the platform that enforces that promise: the infrastructure it runs on, the modules it is made of, and the data model where the pseudonymity guarantee is structural rather than a policy.

01 / Technical architecture

Two applications, one database, four outside services

A deliberately small estate. The web app renders every screen on the server and holds no database credentials; the API is the only thing that talks to Postgres, and the only thing that reaches outside. Nothing in the diagram below is a managed abstraction we cannot inspect — when the research feed slowed to five seconds, the cause was one shared CPU, and we could see it.

This is the prove phase: Fly.io in London, chosen for speed of iteration. The migration target is AWS eu-west-2, timed for the point we open beyond the invited peer group.

A modular monolith, one module per domain, with typed SQL rather than a query builder that hides the plan. Migrations are forward-only and run by the release command.
Postmark

Sign-in links and codes, verification outcomes, replies, kudos and moderation notices. Bounces feed a suppression list; reserved domains are refused before sending.

Two free public corpora, ingested twice daily and deep-paged for history. Deduplicated on DOI, classified against the clinical taxonomy on arrival.
HCPC register

The UK statutory register for allied health professionals. No API, so a rate-limited parse of its own public search — the check that makes "verified-only" true.
02 / Application architecture

Sixteen modules, two rails, and a boundary every request crosses

One deployable, divided by domain rather than by layer: each module owns its tables, its rules and its HTTP surface. The shape matters commercially — it is what lets the research feed be rebuilt twice in a fortnight without touching moderation, and what makes the eventual extraction of a worker service a deployment change rather than a rewrite.

Two things apply to everything, so they are drawn as rails rather than repeated as arrows: every request passes the authentication and app-access guards, and every tunable threshold is read from one settings table at request time.

03 / Data architecture

Four schemas, and one column between a pseudonym and a person

The schema names are the access policy. identity holds who people really are; community holds everything they say, attributed only to a handle; research holds the literature, which belongs to nobody; config holds the operational dials and the audit trail.

Children are drawn indented under their parent — that elbow is a foreign key. The two lines that cross the dashed boundary are the entire surface on which a real name can be reached from pseudonymous activity, and both are there by design.

Why this is the defensible part

The product promise — that ideas win on merit because nobody knows your grade — is only as good as the thing enforcing it. Here it is enforced by reachability. No community query can resolve a real name, because the join does not exist in that half of the schema. The two places that can are reached through separate gates, and every traversal writes an immutable row to identity.identity_access_log with the moderator, the reason, the timestamp and the action taken.

The same reasoning covers clinical safety. Case discussions are published only after a de-identification checklist and a recorded attestation, and the attestation lives on the identity side, bound to the verified person, while the case itself carries only the handle. The platform never stores identifiable patient data; the legal basis is UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018 and the common-law duty of confidentiality.
