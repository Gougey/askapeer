Recreational runner, 3 weeks post lateral ankle sprain — persistent instability despite full ROM. Push proprioception or image?

Presenting complaint
Persistent subjective instability after a grade II lateral ankle sprain. Full weight-bearing achieved.
Patient (de-identified)
Male, age band 30–39. Recreational runner, ~30km/week pre-injury.
Timeline
3 weeks post-injury. Inversion mechanism on uneven ground.
Objective findings
ROM restored and symmetrical. Anterior drawer mild laxity, no bony tenderness (Ottawa negative at presentation). Single-leg balance markedly reduced vs contralateral.
Question to the community
Progress proprioceptive loading and hold off imaging, or is the persistent giving-way a flag to image now?

TTTibiaTraveller
Best answer
Full ROM at 3 weeks with negative Ottawa at presentation — I'd hold off imaging and treat the giving-way as a neuromuscular control deficit, not a structural one. Single-leg balance asymmetry is doing the talking here. Progress proprioceptive loading under fatigue and on unstable surfaces; re-assess at 2 weeks. If instability persists with a stiffening block or night pain, then image.
SSSilentScapula
Agree on delaying imaging. One addition: check peroneal reaction time specifically, not just static balance. Plenty of these feel "unstable" because of delayed peroneal firing rather than true mechanical laxity — and that's very trainable.
QMQuietMoraine
Contrarian view worth considering: if he's a runner and keen to return, a one-off ultrasound is cheap reassurance and rules out an ATFL avulsion you can't feel on drawer. Not because I expect pathology — because it changes his confidence, which is half the rehab battle.
