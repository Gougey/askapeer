# Search abbreviations — what still needs your decision

**For:** Andrew to review · **Drafted:** 21 October 2026 · **Revised:** 5 November 2026 · **Status:** the agreed pairs are built; the rows below are waiting for you

You were sent 324 short form ↔ full term pairs. Of the 291 we proposed to include, 285 are now **built into the app** (the other six are in section 1): typing *rehab* finds papers that say *rehabilitation*, *ACL* finds *anterior cruciate ligament*, and so on, in Discussions and My Research. This page now shows only the **36 rows that need a decision from you**. Section 3 is new: 31 sets of same-named tags that need your view.

**One change from the draft.** The draft said searching either form would find papers using either. That is still true for alternative *names* (Patrick’s test ↔ FABER test), but an *abbreviation* now works one way only: typing **ACL** also finds “anterior cruciate ligament”, while typing *anterior cruciate ligament* is unchanged. The other direction added almost nothing, because papers spell an abbreviation out beside it, and it was where the wrong matches came from: search ignores capitals, so linking TENS back made *transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation* also find every paper that says “tens of thousands”. So the question for each row below is just: **should typing the short form also find papers on the full term?**

**How to reply:** for each row, *include* or *leave out*. Anything you don’t mention stays out. And please add any shorthand you and your colleagues type that is missing; adding a pair later is a small change, not a rebuild.

The numbers are from the papers on live on 21 October. *“3,171 → 13,949”* means typing the short form finds 3,171 papers today and would find 13,949 with the link.

## 3. Tags that share a name

79 tag names are used by more than one tag, 178 tags in all. They look identical on a discussion card but are separate tags, so someone who picks one Foot tag won't find a discussion tagged with the other. Adrian hit the same kind of mismatch with Knee Joint and Knee joints.

**What we propose:** add a short label to each, shown only on discussion cards and in the tag picker, such as *Foot (muscles)* and *Foot (conditions)*. The tags themselves don't change, so how papers are matched stays exactly the same. The labels make the choice clear, but the tags stay separate: a discussion tagged *Foot (conditions)* still won't appear under *Foot (muscles)*.

**What we need from you:** this is the one part of this section that needs your judgement. The **31 names in the first table** look like the same structure listed more than once. For each, tell us whether to keep them apart with the label shown, or merge them into one tag. The 48 in the second table should need nothing, unless a label reads wrongly to you.

Labels can go in straight away. Merging is a bigger taxonomy change that we would plan separately, so any you mark *merge* use the proposed label in the meantime.
